The Boals approach

Not a number in isolation. A conclusion you can understand.

Strong valuation work starts by identifying the real question, then making the path from evidence to conclusion visible.

  • Purpose-built scope around the intended use, users, property rights, effective date, and reporting needs.
  • Market-grounded research with relevant evidence and explicit assumptions.
  • Clear communication before, during, and after delivery when the assignment calls for context.
Why clients choose our model

A disciplined path

From assignment question to usable answer.

Every property is different. The underlying discipline should not be.

  1. Define the assignment

    Property, interest, effective date, intended use and users, timing, and reporting needs.

  2. Build the evidence

    Property documents, market activity, comparable data, and relevant economic context.

  3. Test the conclusion

    Applicable approaches, explicit assumptions, reconciliation, and internal consistency.

  4. Communicate clearly

    A report designed around the assignment—with the reasoning visible, not buried.

Common questions

A clearer start to the appraisal conversation.

Basic property and assignment facts are enough for an initial request. Details can be confirmed before engagement.

What information is helpful when requesting a commercial appraisal proposal?

Start with the property location and type, the decision the appraisal will support, the intended users, the relevant effective date, and the timing requirement. Supporting records can be discussed after the initial request.

Where does The Boals Group provide commercial appraisal services?

The Boals Group's stated service area covers all 62 counties in Tennessee's Western and Middle Grand Divisions. Assignment fit is confirmed before engagement.

What property information should I include?

Share the property type if known—such as office, retail, industrial, multifamily, land, or another commercial use—along with the address and the purpose of the appraisal.
