A valuation is only useful when it answers the right question.

Scope before product

The same property can require very different valuation work.

A lender evaluating collateral, an attorney preparing a case, an owner considering a sale, and an executor documenting an estate may all be looking at the same building. They are not asking the same question.

That is why we begin with the intended use and users, effective date, property interest, and required level of reporting—then propose the work.

01

Financing & credit

Valuation built for a credit decision.

Commercial appraisal and review assignments can support new originations, renewals, collateral monitoring, participation interests, and special-assets work. The scope begins with the institution's intended use, intended users, property rights, and applicable requirements.

  • Commercial appraisal
  • Appraisal review
  • Portfolio and collateral support
  • Proposed construction or renovation
02

Acquisition & disposition

Clear valuation context before a property changes hands.

A purchase price and a supportable opinion of value answer different questions. Valuation and advisory work can help owners, investors, and advisers evaluate a purchase, sale, contribution, exchange, or internal transfer with a clearer view of the real estate.

  • Acquisition due diligence
  • Disposition planning
  • Internal transfers
  • Partial interests and property rights
03

Litigation & dispute

Analysis that can be explained under scrutiny.

Disputes demand more than a final number. They require a carefully defined question, transparent assumptions, relevant evidence, and a conclusion that can be followed by attorneys, clients, and other reviewers.

  • Real property disputes
  • Partition and ownership matters
  • Damages-related valuation
  • Consulting and appraisal review
04

Estate, gift & succession

The right value for the right date and interest.

Estate and planning assignments often turn on an historical effective date, a specific ownership interest, or documentation needs established by the client's legal and tax advisers. Those details belong in the scope from the beginning.

  • Estate and date-of-death valuation
  • Gift planning
  • Trust and fiduciary matters
  • Succession and internal planning
05

Property tax matters

A grounded look at the value behind the assessment.

Owners and advisers may need market-based analysis to understand whether an assessed value is supported. The valuation question, relevant tax date, property facts, and appeal requirements should be confirmed before the work is scoped.

  • Assessment review support
  • Commercial property valuation
  • Market and property research
  • Adviser-ready documentation
06

Public purpose & development

Real estate analysis for change, acquisition, and stewardship.

Public and development assignments may involve a whole property, a partial acquisition, an easement, proposed improvements, or competing uses. A tailored scope keeps the analysis focused on the actual property rights and decision.

  • Eminent domain and right of way
  • Easements and partial acquisitions
  • Highest and best use
  • Market, rent, and feasibility studies

Scoping questions

What a proposal needs to define.

Property facts, intended use, timing, and reporting requirements shape the assignment and the proposal.

How is a commercial appraisal fee determined?

The fee depends on the property, rights appraised, intended use and users, effective date, complexity, research requirements, reporting format, and timing. A proposal follows review of the assignment details.

Does an appraisal review produce a new opinion of value?

Not necessarily. A review can focus on the work under review or include additional analysis. Any separate value opinion must be expressly included in the agreed review scope.
